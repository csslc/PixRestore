01 / Abstract

A restoration-native diffusion model, without the latent bottleneck.

Abstract

Unified image restoration (UIR) aims to recover high-quality (HQ) content from low-quality (LQ) images with different degradations using a single model. Most recent methods adapt large pretrained text-to-image (T2I) latent diffusion models because of their strong capacity and generative priors; however, the variational autoencoder (VAE) in latent T2I models can remove restoration-sensitive details, and the open-ended synthesis prior may introduce content-inconsistent artifacts. We present PixRestore, the VAE-free pixel-space Diffusion Transformer (DiT) for UIR. Its diffusion backbone is trained entirely from scratch, without relying on T2I pretraining. PixRestore performs flow matching directly on patchified pixels, thereby preserving fine-grained details while keeping the token sequence tractable. To adapt to different degradations, PixRestore learns to predict the reliability of layer features using LQ–HQ DINO feature similarity. Features from more reliable layers are fused to form dense conditioning, while the less reliable layers receive stronger supervision from the corresponding HQ features to better remove degradation. We train PixRestore on a large-scale corpus of diverse scenes and degradations, and further finetune it into a one-step generator using DINO-based adversarial objectives for efficient inference. Experiments on public benchmarks and real-world test sets show that, with only about 50M parameters and single-step inference of about 44 ms, PixRestore achieves the best overall fidelity, perceptual quality, and robustness to degradations among previous UIR models while being far more efficient. Larger PixRestore variants further boost performance, demonstrating the scalability of our pixel-space design.
